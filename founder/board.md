# Board of Directors — verdict

Three members, each applying one published framework from
`.claude/skills/founder-board/lenses.md`. The board applies the frameworks; it
does not speak as their authors.

**Reviewed on an incomplete file.** The website was unreachable, so there is no
copy, no proof, no competitor set and no location. Two of three votes are
conditional on that evidence.

---

## Member 1 — the Offers lens

**Market score**

| test | verdict |
| --- | --- |
| Massive pain | **Strong.** Prediabetes and type 2 diabetes are a daily, frightening, medically-reinforced problem. Far more urgent than "get in shape". |
| Buyers have money | **Weakest point.** In the US this population skews older and lower-income. $300–400/month is a real stretch for much of it. The subset who can pay exists but is narrower than the headline market. |
| Easy to find | **Strong.** These buyers self-identify — an A1C number, a doctor's warning, a diagnosis date. Few markets are this targetable. |
| Growing | **Strong**, regrettably. |

**Value equation**

- *Dream outcome*: very high. "Get off medication" beats "lose 20 lbs" every time.
- *Perceived likelihood*: **unknown, and this is what decides the business.** A buyer choosing between you and their endocrinologist needs proof. Client A1C changes, before/after, credentials. I have none.
- *Time to result*: favourable — metabolic markers move in 8–12 weeks and are measurable.
- *Effort*: favourable if the app genuinely reduces the work.

**The pricing note.** At $300 you are priced like generic online coaching and
positioned like clinical support. That is the worst of both. Either look like
generic coaching and charge $150, or own the metabolic claim and charge $400+.
The CFO model says $300 at 30 clients loses $10,575 in year one and $400 makes
$10,594. This lens says the same thing from the demand side: the middle is empty.

**Vote: FUND IF** — if there is documented client proof. Without it this is a
premium claim with nothing behind it.

## Member 2 — the Monopoly lens

**Is the market crowded?** Online fitness coaching is one of the most
commoditized markets in existence — thousands of coaches, near-zero switching
costs, price collapsing toward $100/month.

**But the framing saves it.** "Online fitness coaching" is the wrong market.
"Metabolic coaching for people with a diabetes diagnosis, delivered by a trainer
who works alongside their doctor" is a far smaller market — and a small market
you can dominate is exactly the right starting shape. The narrowing is the asset.
Do not widen it.

**The seven questions, where answerable**

- *Engineering*: incremental today. The app is the only thing that could be a
  step change, and it does not exist yet.
- *Why now*: genuine. GLP-1 drugs have made metabolic health mainstream and
  created a large population who need coaching alongside medication. That is a
  real opening, and it is open now.
- *Monopoly*: possible in a tightly-drawn niche. Not possible in "fitness".
- *Distribution*: **unanswered, and this is the one that kills solo coaching
  businesses.** $1,200/month of ads must produce 3–4 clients a month. Nothing I
  have shows a repeatable channel.
- *Durability*: **weak.** A solo coach has no moat. You are the moat, which means
  there isn't one. The app and accumulated outcome data are the only candidates.
- *The secret*: unknown. If the founder has personally reversed their own
  metabolic markers, or has a protocol that produced repeatable A1C drops, that
  is the secret, the proof and the marketing in one. It should be the loudest
  thing on the site.

**What stops a copycat in month six?** Today: nothing. That is the finding.

**Vote: FUND IF** — conditional on a named distribution channel with a real cost
per client, and a stated plan for what becomes the moat.

## Member 3 — the Product lens

**The one thing — and a conflict in the name.**

"Metabolic GFC Xtreme Fit" contains two businesses pulling opposite ways:

- *Metabolic* says clinical, measured, sustainable, doctor-adjacent. Its buyer is
  52, recently diagnosed, frightened, and has been told to be careful.
- *Xtreme* says bootcamp, intensity, push harder. Its buyer is 28 and wants to be
  pushed.

**A newly-diagnosed 52-year-old is not shopping for "Xtreme."** The word does
active damage to the exact buyer the rest of the name is trying to attract. It
also undercuts the premium price, because "Xtreme" reads as bootcamp, and
bootcamps are cheap.

This is the single highest-leverage change available, it costs nothing, and it
does not require a website to see.

**Work back from the experience.** The moment that matters is a buyer seeing a
number move — an A1C or fasting glucose reading going down, attributed to the
programme. Everything should be built back from that moment. If the app does one
thing, it should be making that number visible and attributable. Workout logging
is a commodity; tracking metabolic markers against a protocol is not.

**What to cut.** Anything that makes this look like general fitness coaching.
"Xtreme" first.

**One-sentence test.** It does not pass yet. Compare: "I help people with a
recent type 2 diagnosis get their A1C down without living in a gym."

**Vote: FUND IF** — conditional on resolving the positioning conflict.

---

## Board summary

**Verdict: FUND WITH CONDITIONS (3 of 3 conditional, 0 pass, 0 unconditional)**

The market is real, the timing is genuinely good, and the niche is narrow enough
to own. The business is undermined by its own positioning and has no proven way
to acquire customers.

### Conditions — the checklist for the rest of the pack

1. **Produce the proof.** Client results, A1C changes, credentials, testimonials.
   Blocks the premium price. → `/founder-offer`
2. **Resolve the name.** "Metabolic" and "Xtreme" cannot share a label. Pick the
   buyer, then the word. → `/founder-brand`
3. **Name the channel.** One repeatable source of clients with a measured cost
   per client. 30 clients at 8% churn means selling 45. → `/founder-marketing`
4. **Decide the price.** $300 is the dead middle. The CFO says $400 is the
   difference between a loss and a profit at 30 clients. → `/founder-pricing`
5. **Get the scope-of-practice review.** A trainer coaching diagnosed diabetics
   sits close to a line that varies by state and country. What may be claimed,
   what requires physician sign-off, what insurance is needed. **Not legal
   advice — a professional must confirm this before launch.** → `/founder-ops`
6. **Name the moat.** Today a copycat is six months away. → `/founder-plan`
